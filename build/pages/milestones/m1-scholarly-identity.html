Scholarly Identity Statement

In about 500 words, say in your own voice who you are as a scholar: which questions drive you, what animates your research, and why it matters. The statement is an account of intellectual purpose, written from conviction.

The prompt

Who are you as a scholar? What do you care about, what animates your research, and why does it matter? Where did your intellectual questions come from, and what do you want your work to do in the world?

Some ways in. Use them as entry points, and skip any which do not help.

  • What brought you to your research questions? What is the origin story?
  • What question keeps you up at night?
  • What would be lost if your research did not exist?
  • With whom is your work in conversation, and whom is it for?
  • If a skeptical colleague asked why it matters, what would you say?

What the statement is

A strong statement names things. It reads as the work of a person who cares about something, and it explains why the work matters beyond the field. Four kinds of writing fall short of the threshold: a summary of your CV or application materials, a list of topics, a statement written for a search committee, and a description of what you plan to study.

Check your draft

Read the statement aloud before you submit it, then ask the questions below.

Assessment

Complete and substantive
States intellectual purpose and why the research matters, with conviction and specificity.
Nearly there
Engages the prompt but reads as written for a committee, leaves the stakes unclear, or reads as a topic list. Revision expected.
Not yet complete
Not submitted, or below the expectations for doctoral reflection. Come talk to me.

Submitting

Generative AI

Generative AI is permitted for brainstorming or light editing. The substance of the statement must be yours, in your voice, from your own experience and convictions. The reason is practical. The statement exists to surface what you think and want, and no model has access to either. If you are unsure whether a use fits, ask me before you submit.

Questions go to kkcole2@ncsu.edu, or bring them to my office before class. If the statement is hard to write, the difficulty is worth a conversation.
