Before class

Milestone due: Draft Exam List

A working draft of your comprehensive exam list with a one-paragraph rationale. The rationale states the argument of the list: why these texts, in this configuration, for this project. It also names the fields your dissertation will answer to and the position from which you enter each one. A disciplinary list locates a position within one field. An interdisciplinary list maps where several fields meet your problem and states which field claims the project first. The list need not be final, but it must be real. 25% of the final grade. Submit through Canvas, by email, or in hard copy.

Full guide to the Draft Exam List milestone

Bring

  • Your draft list and rationale, ready to trade with a partner.
  • Any responses from prospective committee members.

Questions for peer review

  • What intellectual logic holds the list together: theme, method, genealogy, intervention?
  • Where are the temporal, geographic, or demographic gaps? Which fields or traditions are absent?
  • Does the list reflect what the field values, what you value, or what your committee values, and do the three agree?
  • Which fields will read this dissertation, and from what position does the list enter each?
  • Is there a text on the list you have not read? What would a list you wanted to read look like?

Structured peer review

Pairs are assigned. Each reader responds in writing before any discussion begins.

  1. Exchange lists and rationale paragraphs.
  2. Respond in writing for fifteen minutes: the organizing logic I see, a gap I notice, a text I would add and why, and the most interesting tension on the list.
  3. Discuss the responses, ten minutes per list.
  4. Join the full group to compare what the lists reveal about the cohort.
