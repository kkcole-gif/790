Arrivals

Welcome to CRDM 790. This first session is for meeting each other as thinkers, so there is nothing to read, and the only preparation is a short piece of writing about where you come from. Bring yourself and your questions.

The first session introduces the cohort to itself. Each of you arrives with an intellectual history, and the work of the semester begins by putting those histories in the room.

Before we meet

Read

  • No required reading. If you want an early start, the two Giroux essays for Week 2 are linked on the Week 2 page.

Read with a pen. The marginalia guide describes the practice.

Write and bring

  • If you have one, a book you have marked up. We open our conversation about marginalia with it.
  • An intellectual autobiography, 200 to 300 words and informal. Where did you start, what changed you, and where are you now? You will share it, and no one reads your writing aloud without your consent.

Questions to arrive with

  • What brought you to this program, and what do you hope this year makes possible?
  • What question keeps you up at night, the one you keep returning to?
  • Who taught you how to think? How would you describe your intellectual lineage?
  • What does becoming a scholar mean to you at this moment?

Constellation mapping

A first picture of the cohort, built from index cards. The cards return in Week 15.

  1. Write your name in the center of a large index card. Around it, write a discipline, a method, a thinker, a question, and one surprise: something about you outside your official biography.
  2. Post the cards on the wall and walk the gallery in silence for five minutes.
  3. As a group, name the patterns, the surprises, and the gaps.
  4. Hand your card to me. You will see it again at the end of the semester.

After we meet

  • Read Giroux for Week 2, pen in hand. The marginalia guide explains the practice.
  • Sign up for a reading lead slot in Canvas.
  • Book an office hours conversation if you want to talk through your plans for the year.
