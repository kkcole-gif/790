Full guide to the Prospectus workshop draft milestone

Write and bring

  • Your complete draft.
  • A one-paragraph director’s note: what you are least sure of, where you most need feedback, and what feels strong. Your group reads it first.

The response protocol

  • The project as I understand it: describe it back to the writer in two or three sentences.
  • The strongest moment: quote the sentence where the stakes are clearest.
  • The murkiest moment: name the exact place where you lost the thread.
  • The missing piece: what question does the prospectus raise without answering?
  • One concrete suggestion: specific and actionable, such as “the third paragraph needs an example of ___.”

Prospectus workshop

Groups are assigned. Each draft receives the same structure, timed strictly.

  1. Silent reading of the draft and its director’s note, if the group has not read it in advance.
  2. Written responses using the protocol.
  3. Discussion, with the writer listening before speaking.
  4. The writer responds: what did you hear, what surprised you, and what will you take away?

After we meet

  • Make the revision you named.
  • Prepare a five to ten minute presentation of your project for Week 15.

Further reading, if this week opened something up

  • Joan Bolker, Writing Your Dissertation in Fifteen Minutes a Day, on sustainable writing practice.
