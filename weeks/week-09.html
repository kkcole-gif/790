Movement II · Week 9 · Thursday, March 11

Situated Knowledges: Revising the List

Haraway helps us ask where we stand when we claim to know something. A faculty member who chairs committees joins us, and we audit our lists together. Bring whatever your committee contacts have said.

Kirsti

Read
Haraway, “Situated Knowledges” (about 25 pages)
Bring
Your revised draft list, a standpoint paragraph, and any committee responses
Guest
A program faculty member who chairs committees
Due
No milestone this week

Haraway argues against the view from nowhere. This week applies her argument to your list, alongside a faculty member who reads lists from the committee’s side of the table.

Before we meet

Read

  • Donna Haraway, “Situated Knowledges: The Science Question in Feminism and the Privilege of Partial Perspective,” Feminist Studies 14, no. 3 (1988): 575–599. In the week’s Canvas Files folder, or on Library Reserve.

Read with a pen. The marginalia guide describes the practice.

Write and bring

  • Your draft list, revised after peer review.
  • One paragraph on where you stand when you claim to know your problem, drawing on Haraway.
  • Any responses from prospective committee members, with a note on what each changed.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • Haraway argues against the view from nowhere. From where does your list claim to know, and does its structure show it?
  • Which fields will read your dissertation first? Which will read it skeptically?
  • If your project is interdisciplinary, which field claims it first, and what does the claim cost the others?
  • If your project sits within one discipline, which position in the field does your list take, and which does it leave out?
  • What did your committee contacts say about the list, and what did their responses show about where they stand?

Canon audit of the draft list

The audit works on your own draft. Auditing an imagined canon describes a field. Auditing your own list asks you to decide what to change.

  1. Work from your Week 8 draft, revised after peer review.
  2. For each text, note the author’s apparent position, as far as you know it. Mark texts centering Global South, feminist, queer, or disabled perspectives.
  3. As a group, describe the demographic and disciplinary shape of the lists this cohort has drafted.
  4. Name one text absent from your list which belongs there, and one text you will cut. Both changes go into the list you bring to your individual meeting.

After we meet

  • Book your individual meeting for the March 11 to 24 window.
  • Confirm your chair, or draft the plan Milestone 3 asks for.

Further reading, if this week opened something up

  • Patricia Hill Collins, Black Feminist Thought, on standpoint epistemology and the matrix of domination.
  • Kimberlé Crenshaw, “Mapping the Margins,” the foundational text on intersectionality.
