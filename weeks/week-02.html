Movement I · Week 2 · Thursday, January 21

Critical Pedagogy and the Scholar-Critic

We begin the reading with Giroux because he asks the question this seminar keeps circling: what does it mean to think critically from inside an institution? Annotate honestly, including where you disagree.

Kirsti

Read
Giroux, On Critical Pedagogy, Chapter 1 (about 46 pages)
Bring
A Perusall annotation pair and one example from your own education
Due
No milestone this week

Giroux argues education is never neutral. This week asks what it means to be a critic inside an institution which grades, funds, and credentials you.

Before we meet

Read

  • Henry Giroux, On Critical Pedagogy, 2nd ed. (Bloomsbury, 2020). Chapter 1, “Critical Pedagogy and the Postmodern/Modern Divide,” pages 17 to 63. On Perusall, and available online through the library.
  • Optional: Chapter 2, “Cultural Studies, Critical Pedagogy, and the Politics of Higher Education,” pages 65 to 113.

Write and bring

  • Two Perusall annotations: one passage you agree with and one you push back on, with two or three sentences of response on each.
  • One concrete example from your own education which confirms or complicates Giroux’s argument.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • What does it mean to be a critic inside an institution? How do you critique the hand which grades you?
  • Giroux holds education either domesticates or liberates. Do you agree? Where have you experienced either?
  • What does it mean to be educated inside a university which treats you as human capital?
  • What does critical pedagogy ask of you, as a student and as a future teacher?
  • Where do you feel the tension between institutional belonging and intellectual dissent most sharply?

Institutional autopsy

An inventory of the rules of academic life we follow without examining them.

  1. In pairs, each of you names one rule, norm, or unspoken expectation of academic life you have accepted without question.
  2. Share them with the room. I list them on the board without comment.
  3. As a group, decide which Giroux would call hegemonic, which are useful, and which deserve breaking.
  4. Write one sentence: “The assumption I am most committed to interrogating this semester is ___.”

After we meet

  • Read Benjamin for Week 3, and choose the object you will bring.

Further reading, if this week opened something up

  • bell hooks, Teaching to Transgress, more direct than Giroux on the embodied experience of the critical classroom.
  • Stuart Hall, “Cultural Studies and Its Theoretical Legacies,” on intellectual formation and the cost of thinking critically inside institutions.
