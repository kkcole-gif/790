Movement II · Week 7 · Thursday, February 25

Hauntology: What Haunts Your Field?

Fisher asks what keeps returning in a culture without resolution. Bring the haunting you notice in your field, and a first list of ten texts. Neither needs polish.

Kirsti

Read
Fisher, “What Is Hauntology?” and “The Slow Cancellation of the Future”
Bring
A one-page haunting reflection and a first-pass exam list of ten texts
Due
No milestone this week

Fisher takes Derrida’s hauntology into music, television, and the felt sense of a future which failed to arrive. This week asks what keeps returning in your field, unresolved, and whether naming it gives you a project.

Before we meet

Read

  • Mark Fisher, “What Is Hauntology?” Film Quarterly 66, no. 1 (2012): 16–24. On JSTOR through NC State Libraries, with no limit on readers.
  • Mark Fisher, “The Slow Cancellation of the Future,” the opening essay of Ghosts of My Life: Writings on Depression, Hauntology and Lost Futures (Zero Books, 2014). Read the opening essay, which ends where “Ghosts of My Life: Goldie, Japan, Tricky” begins.
  • Read the article first. It sets out the terms, from Derrida’s specter to the music Fisher hears as haunted. The essay then turns the concept on the present, and on Fisher himself.

Read with a pen. The marginalia guide describes the practice.

Write and bring

  • A haunting reflection, about one page and informal. Name one haunting in your field: something it keeps circling without resolving. What is it, and why does it return? Post it to Canvas or bring it to class.
  • Your marked copy, with a note beside the concept in Fisher most generative for your research, and a tentative application to your own work.
  • A first-pass exam list of ten texts you currently expect on your list. Tag each text with the field it belongs to, and add one sentence on why it belongs. Browse exam lists from past CRDM students for models of scope and organization.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • Fisher distinguishes the no longer from the not yet: what has ceased to be but persists, and what has not yet happened but already shapes the present. Which describes the haunting in your field?
  • The slow cancellation of the future runs through Fisher: cultural possibilities foreclosed without being resolved. What lost future haunts your discipline?
  • Fisher reads hauntology through music and television. Which of your field’s objects would carry the same reading, and what does the translation require?
  • Is hauntology a method or a mood? How would you use it analytically, beyond atmosphere?

Rapid presentations

Three minutes each, no slides. The limit keeps the presentations honest and the energy up.

  1. Present your haunting.
  2. One listener offers a spectral connection: does this haunting appear in their field too, in another form?
  3. As a group, map the hauntings on the board. Look for patterns, shared specters, and divergences.
  4. Discuss which hauntings hold a dissertation project, and how naming a haunting changes your relation to it.

After we meet

  • Turn the first-pass list into a working draft with a rationale for Week 8 peer review. The graded list is due March 24.
  • Milestone 2, Committee Confirmation, is due before Week 8: your chair’s confirmation, or a plan with names and dates.

Further reading, if this week opened something up

  • Merlin Coverley, Hauntology: Ghosts of Futures Past (Oldcastle Books, 2020), a genealogy of the concept from Marx and Dickens to Fisher.
  • Jacques Derrida, Specters of Marx, where the concept begins. In the Readings folder.
  • Saidiya Hartman, Wayward Lives, Beautiful Experiments, a model of writing with the archive’s silences.
  • Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, “Paranoid Reading and Reparative Reading.” Is hauntology paranoid or reparative, and which do you tend toward?
