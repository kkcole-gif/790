Movement II · Week 7 · Thursday, February 25

Hauntology: What Haunts Your Field?

Coverley traces hauntology from Derrida through Fisher into music, literature, and landscape. This week asks what keeps returning in your field, unresolved, and whether naming it gives you a project.

Read
Coverley, Hauntology: Ghosts of Futures Past, Parts 1 and 3
Bring
A one-page haunting reflection and a first-pass exam list of ten texts
Due
No milestone this week

Before class

Read

  • Merlin Coverley, Hauntology: Ghosts of Futures Past (Reaktion Books, 2023), Parts 1 and 3. Page ranges on Perusall.
  • Part 1 gives the history and theory, from Derrida’s specter to Fisher’s lost futures. Part 3 shows the method at work across cultural forms, and it matters most for anyone working across research domains.

Write and bring

  • A haunting reflection, about one page and informal. Name one haunting in your field: something it keeps circling without resolving. What is it, and why does it return? Post it to Canvas or bring it to class.
  • A Perusall annotation on the thinker or concept in Coverley most generative for your research, with a tentative application to your own work.
  • A first-pass exam list of ten texts you currently imagine on your list. Tag each text with the field it belongs to, and add one sentence on why it belongs.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • Coverley moves from Derrida through Fisher into contemporary culture. Which moment in his genealogy feels most alive for your field, and why?
  • The lost future runs through Coverley: cultural possibilities foreclosed without being resolved. What lost future haunts your discipline?
  • Coverley maps hauntology across music, literature, and landscape. Which of these domains translates best into your field, and what does the translation require?
  • Is hauntology a method or a mood? How would you use it analytically, beyond atmosphere?

Rapid presentations

Three minutes each, no slides. The limit keeps the presentations honest and the energy up.

  1. Present your haunting.
  2. One listener offers a spectral connection: does this haunting appear in their field too, in another form?
  3. As a group, map the hauntings on the board. Look for patterns, shared specters, and divergences.
  4. Discuss which hauntings hold a dissertation project, and how naming a haunting changes your relation to it.

After class

  • Turn the first-pass list into a draft exam list with a rationale, due before Week 8.
  • Keep any replies from committee contacts. They inform the list.

Further reading

  • Mark Fisher, Ghosts of My Life, the richest extended application of hauntology to contemporary culture. Read it after Coverley.
  • Jacques Derrida, Specters of Marx, where the concept begins.
  • Saidiya Hartman, Wayward Lives, Beautiful Experiments, a model of writing with the archive’s silences.
  • Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, “Paranoid Reading and Reparative Reading.” Is hauntology paranoid or reparative, and which do you tend toward?
