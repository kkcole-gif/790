Movement I · Week 4 · Thursday, February 4

Who Gets to Live

Mbembe extends Foucault’s biopolitics to ask who is allowed to live and who is made to die, by design. This week tests his concepts against examples you bring.

Read
Mbembe, Necropolitics, Introduction and Chapter 3 (about 41 pages)
Bring
A Perusall annotation and one example of necropolitical logic
Due
No milestone this week

Before class

Read

  • Achille Mbembe, Necropolitics, trans. Steve Corcoran (Duke University Press, 2019). Introduction, pages 1 to 14, and Chapter 3, “Necropolitics,” pages 66 to 92. On Perusall, and available online through the library.
  • The reading is short and dense. Give it two sittings.

Write and bring

  • A Perusall annotation, three or four sentences, applying one of Mbembe’s concepts to your research area.
  • One example, from media, policy, your archive, or your community, which you think shows necropolitical logic at work. Be ready to put it on the table.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • Mbembe distinguishes making live, letting die, and making die. How does the distinction change how you think about power?
  • For Mbembe, necropolitics describes actual bodies in actual places. Where do you see its logic in what you study?
  • What would change in your project if you foregrounded the bodies it touches?
  • Mbembe writes from a specific history and geography. How does his position shape the argument, and how does yours shape your reading?
  • How does Mbembe sit alongside, or complicate, Giroux and Benjamin?

Concept translation workshop

A test of where a theoretical concept holds and where it strains.

  1. In pairs, map Mbembe’s distinction (making live, letting die, making die) onto your partner’s example.
  2. Push each other: is this necropolitical, or does another framework serve the example better?
  3. As a full group, name where the concept held and where it strained, and what the strain shows about the reach of critical theory.
  4. Write one sentence on how Mbembe has shifted, or left unchanged, the way you frame your research problem.

After class

  • Read Brown for Week 5.

Further reading

  • Frantz Fanon, The Wretched of the Earth, with Sartre’s introduction and Bhabha’s preface, foundational for postcolonial and critical race frameworks.
  • Christina Sharpe, In the Wake, which extends Mbembe into Black Studies.
