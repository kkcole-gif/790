Movement I · Week 4 · Thursday, February 4

Who Gets to Live

Mbembe is short and demanding, so give him two sittings. Ruha Benjamin then carries his questions into the technologies many of you study. The examples you bring will carry much of the conversation, so choose one you care about.

Kirsti

Read
Mbembe, Necropolitics, Introduction and Chapter 3, and Ruha Benjamin, Race After Technology, Introduction (about 80 pages)
Bring
Your marked copy and one example of necropolitical logic
Due
No milestone this week

Mbembe extends Foucault’s biopolitics to ask who is allowed to live and who is made to die, by design. This week tests his concepts against examples you bring.

Before we meet

Read

  • Achille Mbembe, Necropolitics, trans. Steve Corcoran (Duke University Press, 2019). Introduction, “The Ordeal of the World,” pages 1 to 8, and Chapter 3, “Necropolitics,” pages 66 to 92. Both open through NC State Libraries on JSTOR, with no limit on readers. Sign in with your Unity ID and download the chapter PDF.
  • Ruha Benjamin, Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code, Introduction, “The New Jim Code” (Polity, 2019).
  • Read Mbembe first, in two sittings. Then read Benjamin for how a design choice becomes a decision about whose life counts.

Read with a pen. The marginalia guide describes the practice.

Write and bring

  • Your marked copy, with three or four sentences in the margin applying one of Mbembe’s concepts to your research area.
  • One example, from media, policy, your archive, or your community, which you think shows necropolitical logic at work. Be ready to put it on the table.

Questions to carry into the reading

  • Mbembe distinguishes making live, letting die, and making die. How does the distinction change how you think about power?
  • For Mbembe, necropolitics describes actual bodies in actual places. Where do you see its logic in what you study?
  • What would change in your project if you foregrounded the bodies it touches?
  • Mbembe writes from a specific history and geography. How does his position shape the argument, and how does yours shape your reading?
  • Ruha Benjamin names a New Jim Code: technologies which encode inequity while appearing neutral. Where does her account meet Mbembe’s, and where does it part from it?
  • How do Mbembe and Ruha Benjamin sit alongside, or complicate, Giroux and Walter Benjamin?

Concept translation workshop

A test of where a theoretical concept holds and where it strains.

  1. In pairs, map Mbembe’s distinction (making live, letting die, making die) onto your partner’s example.
  2. Push each other: is this necropolitical, or does another framework serve the example better?
  3. As a full group, name where the concept held and where it strained, and what the strain shows about the reach of critical theory.
  4. Write one sentence on how Mbembe has shifted, or left unchanged, the way you frame your research problem.

Further reading, if this week opened something up

  • Christina Sharpe, In the Wake, which extends Mbembe into Black Studies.
