Draft Exam List

An exam list makes an argument about what your project needs and where you stand in relation to the fields it draws on. The draft goes to peer review in Week 8 and to a canon audit in Week 9. It need not be complete, but it must be real.

What to submit

Part 1: the draft list

A working list with full citations, organized by the logic your project calls for: theme, method, genealogy, period, or a combination. Mark any section still to be determined.

CRDM lists usually run 50 to 80 texts across all areas. At this stage the structure matters more than the count.

Part 2: the rationale

One substantive paragraph stating the argument of the list: why these texts, in this configuration, prepare you for this project. The rationale also names the fields your dissertation will answer to and the position from which you enter each one.

  • If your project sits within one discipline, locate your position in it. Which conversation does the list join, and which does it set aside?
  • If your project is interdisciplinary, map where the fields meet your problem, and state which field claims the project first.

Falls short: “These texts are foundational to my field and will prepare me for the exam.”

Closer: “My list is organized around three intersecting questions: how digital platforms mediate political subjectivity, how critical geography and STS have theorized surveillance, and what feminist media scholars have argued about algorithmic visibility. I include thinkers from adjacent fields rarely assigned in CRDM courses, because my project needs their vocabulary.”

Peer review in Week 8

Your reviewer responds in writing to five prompts:

  1. The organizing logic I see.
  2. A gap I notice: a tradition, period, perspective, or demographic absent from the list.
  3. A text I would add, and why.
  4. The most interesting tension on the list.
  5. One question for the author about the choices behind it.

You revise after peer review, again after the Week 9 audit, and bring the result to your individual meeting. A rough draft is fine. The workshop fails without one.

Assessment

Complete and substantive
A defensible organizing logic, a rationale which argues, and awareness of what the list includes, excludes, and why.
Nearly there
A list with a descriptive rationale, an unclear logic, or no acknowledgment of gaps. Revision expected before Week 10.
Not yet complete
Not submitted before the workshop, or submitted without a rationale. Come talk to me.
