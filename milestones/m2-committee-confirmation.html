Milestone 2 · Due before class, Week 8 (March 4) · 15% of the final grade

Committee Confirmation

You confirm or complete your four-person committee. Some of you arrive with a committee already listed on your Plan of Work, and some arrive without one. Either way, hand in either confirmation from your committee, or a short written plan naming the faculty you will ask, where each conversation stands, and when you expect answers. Either one meets the threshold when it is honest.

What you hand in
A forwarded email from your chair, or a one-page plan
Due
Before class, Thursday, March 4 (Week 8)
Weight
15% of the final grade
Submit
Canvas or email
What happens next
Your committee’s responses shape the exam list you submit on March 24

Why it comes before the exam list

Your committee reads your exam list, examines you on it, and later reads your dissertation. A list built before you know who those readers are answers to no one in particular. So the committee comes first. This order also gives faculty time: February replies run slow, and a conversation started in Week 6 has a month to develop before your list is due.

What the program requires

The CRDM academic policies set the shape of the committee:

  • Four members, including the chair. A fifth member needs the Director of Graduate Programs’ approval.
  • The chair comes from the CRDM core faculty.
  • At least one member from Communication and one from English. Members from outside NC State need Graduate School approval.
  • A Graduate School Representative joins for the oral preliminary exam. The Graduate School assigns this person, who does not vote. You do not recruit one.
  • Timing: the program asks you to name your advisor and committee by the end of your third semester, on the Plan of Work. If yours has changed since, the Plan of Work needs reapproval.

What you hand in

Choose one option. They describe two stages of the same process.

Option A: your chair’s confirmation

Forward or paste the email in which your chair agrees to serve, and list the other three members with a note on who has agreed. If your approved Plan of Work already names the committee and nothing has changed, a screenshot of it with one line confirming each member counts.

Option B: a written plan

A credible plan counts as complete. Most students submit this option. The plan names:

  • All four members, named: the chair, the Communication member, the English member, and the fourth member, with a note on which requirement each fills.
  • The status of each conversation: asked and agreed, asked and waiting, or planning to ask by a named date.
  • A timeline for completing the committee, with dates.
  • One or two sentences per person on why they belong on this committee.

Falls short: “I am thinking about asking a few people in the department. I will reach out soon.”

These example plans are illustrations written for this guide, drawn from three kinds of CRDM projects. Each label names the field, the goal after the degree, and where the questions came from.

Platform and media studies · faculty career

English: Dr. C, for digital rhetoric, asking by March 6 after Dr. A’s introduction. Fourth member: Dr. D in Sociology, asking by March 20, with Graduate School approval if needed. How to get there, step by step

  1. Week 6 (February 18): draft your committee map and first email. We workshop the email in class with a recently advanced student. Send it within 48 hours.
  2. Weeks 6 and 7: meet your likely chair, or your current chair if you have one. Ask how they work with students, how often they meet, and whom they would suggest for the other seats.
  3. Week 7: ask your remaining members, checking the Communication and English requirement as you go.
  4. By March 4: write up where things stand, as Option A or Option B, and submit before class.
  5. After March 4: keep going. Bring your committee’s responses to the Week 9 canon audit and to your Week 10 meeting, which you book on my calendar.

Questions to think through

These questions describe a strong committee. The milestone does not grade them. Use them to choose, and bring them to the Week 8 workshop and your Week 10 meeting.

QuestionA strong answerA weaker answer
Intellectual fitEach member brings something the project needs: a method, a tradition, a subject, an outside discipline.Members chosen because they are available, collegial, or likely to say yes.
Productive challengeAt least one member will push back in ways which improve the work.A committee certain to agree with you.
Your goalsIf your goals include work beyond the academy, the committee speaks to those paths too.A committee oriented only to the academic job market, whatever your plans.
First contactYou have spoken with your chair, or scheduled the conversation.Faculty identified but not yet contacted, without a dated plan.

If you hit obstacles

A faculty member has not replied. You are unsure whom to ask. An advising relationship is difficult. You do not know how to make first contact. Say so in your submission. I reply within the week, and we return to it in your Week 10 meeting. Silence is the one response which leaves the problem in place.

How it is assessed

Complete and substantive
A chair’s confirmation, or a plan with named faculty, the status of each conversation, and a specific timeline. Earns the full 15 points.
Nearly there
A plan with unnamed faculty or a vague timeline. Earns 12 points, and a revision with names and dates earns the full 15.
Not yet complete
Nothing submitted and no word about obstacles. Come talk to me.
