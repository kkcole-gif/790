Dissertation Prospectus

A prospectus argues there is a problem worth investigating, you are positioned to investigate it, and you have a principled method for doing so. It does intellectual work on the page.

What the prospectus does

No section structure is required. Organize the document as your argument requires, as long as all four elements are present and substantive.

The problem
Which question you ask and why it matters. Name the phenomenon, tension, or gap with precision. “I am studying social media” names a topic.
The conversation
Who has taken up the question, and what your project does differently. Make the conversation visible and state your position in it.
The method
How you will pursue the question, and why the approach suits the problem. Argue for the method and its methodology together, as in the Week 11 activity.
The stakes
Why the work matters to scholars and to readers beyond the discipline, stated honestly and specifically.

ScholarshipEngages specific scholars and arguments, and says what your project adds.A list of names, or a description of the field.

StakesConcrete about why the work matters to a community, a public, a policy conversation, or the people it touches.Contributing to the literature, with significance confined to the field.

MomentumConveys direction and conviction about the questions, uncertainty included.Hedged and tentative throughout.

The workshop draft

Upload a complete draft to Canvas, or share it through Google Drive, by 11:59 pm on April 14. Bring printed copies for your group of three or four if you prefer print. Add a one-paragraph director’s note: what you are least sure of, where you most need feedback, and what feels strong.

Your readers respond with the protocol on the Week 14 page. The workshop is generative. Its purpose is to make the draft better.

A nonacademic prospectus

If your post-degree goals are primarily nonacademic, you have the option of writing a public-facing research proposal or a professional research agenda. It asks for the same rigor, applied to a different problem of audience and form. Talk with me before Week 11. Submit it as a document, an annotated outline, a slide deck with notes, or a combination.

Assessment

Complete and substantive
A clear problem, real engagement with scholarship, a justified method, and stakes beyond the field, with independent thinking and momentum.
Nearly there
One or more elements underdeveloped, or a workshop draft too incomplete for useful feedback.
Not yet complete
Not submitted, or below the expectations for doctoral work. Come talk to me right away.

Submitting the final version

Submit by 11:00 am on April 29, the close of our scheduled exam slot (8:30 to 11:00 am), through Canvas (PDF or Word), by email to kkcole2@ncsu.edu, in hard copy, or as a Google Drive link. The earlier you raise a concern, the more help I give.

Generative AI

Generative AI is permitted for brainstorming or editing. The problem, the scholarly positioning, the methodological argument, and the stakes must be yours.
